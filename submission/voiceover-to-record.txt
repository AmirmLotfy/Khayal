Chat sends the spirits. Your shadow guards the light. This is Khayal: a shadow theatre built for Twitch, where the audience takes the puppets and the streamer steps inside the performance.

The idea comes from Egyptian shadow theatre: a lamp, a screen, and figures brought to life by people. Khayal brings that shared performance into a stream, with a camera silhouette and puppets controlled through chat.

A viewer summons a cat or a camel, then moves it with simple commands. Pull it toward the lamp and the shadow grows larger and softer. Each puppet belongs to one viewer, with a queue when the stage fills.

Then comes Afreet Hunt. Sixty seconds. Five lanterns. Chat sends spirits toward the lights while the streamer tries to swat them away with a changing silhouette. Keep at least three lanterns burning to win. The camera reads movement along your outline, so a still shadow is not a hit.

A separate cue book lets the streamer direct the show. Rehearsal is clearly labeled, so simulated events stay separate from Twitch. Captions are cleaned, commands have cooldowns, and stage moderation stays under the broadcaster's control.

When the curtain falls, a playbill credits the cast. The goal is a small shared performance: an unexpected giant cat, a desperate save, and a record of who helped make the night.

I directed the concept and used Codex to build the TypeScript app, command handling, cue book, Twitch connection code, and camera worker. Pixi draws the theatre; MediaPipe supplies the silhouette. Tests check the rules, while real camera and Twitch recordings must prove the performance.

Khayal gives chat a part to play and the streamer a stage to inhabit. Bring your shadow. Guard the light. Come back tomorrow. There is more to see.
